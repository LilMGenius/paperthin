Attack a plan before effort goes into it and return its one load-bearing objection with the cheapest experiment that would prove it.

## Goal

Before real effort is committed, test whether a whole plan, design, or argument holds by asking what an opponent who wants it to fail would hit first. The target is the plan's load-bearing structure as a whole, beyond any single fact or line. The result is one objection plus the cheaper experiment the elaborate plan was hiding, never a checklist. That experiment is the **first nail**: the cheapest falsifying experiment.

## Workflow

1. **Trigger**: the human fires it on a plan, design, or argument, before committing effort.
2. **Find the load-bearing assumptions**: what must be true for the whole to stand.
3. **Attack along every axis that applies.** Always consider the general axes:
   - (a) a load-bearing fact that may be false;
   - (b) confabulation: an after-the-fact story treated as ground truth;
   - (c) analogy taken as isomorphism: assuming structure carries across domains where it does not;
   - (d) future-tense suture: leaning on a result that does not exist yet;
   - (e) the sharpest one: citing a principle while implementing its opposite.
4. **For an empirical or research plan, add two axes:**
   - (f) leakage: no external ground truth enters validation independently;
   - (g) statistical power and family-wise alpha: uncorrected multiple tests make a true hypothesis fail automatically, or a condition with near-zero power approves the result whatever the truth.
5. **Collapse to the root**: the single objection whose failure makes the rest moot.
6. **Find the first nail**: the cheapest experiment that would falsify the load-bearing assumption the root attacks, measured by time, cost, or sample size. It must be able to kill the plan before the expensive program runs.
7. **Return `{ root, first_nail }`**: the root as one sentence, the first nail as one experiment with its cost, and nothing list-shaped.

## Rules

- Attack only; never improve the plan. Improving is a different reflex.
- Own the synthesis: many axes may fire, but the output is one load-bearing root, never a list.
- Always return one root and one first nail; there is no "no objection" result.
- User-invoked on purpose: an attack reflex always within the agent's reach would bias it toward tearing things down. Only the human fires it.

## Verification

1. The root is load-bearing: without it the plan falls.
2. The first nail costs less than the plan it would pre-empt.
